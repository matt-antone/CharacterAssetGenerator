Performance arc

A two-beat side-step-tap, danced square to camera with a constant knee bounce. The cycle opens with the weight already settled over the character-right foot on the balls of both feet, the bounce carrying downward into the beat. Frames 1-3 are the first tap: the character-left foot lifts and crosses behind the character-right, touches, and the weight rocks back to centre as both forearms float up to chest height. Keep the lift small — this is a tap, not a step out.

Frames 4-6 are the transfer: weight passes through centre with the legs nearly straight at the top of the bounce, forearms dropping back to waist height. That straightening is the anticipation for the second tap, so let the knees rebound rather than hold.

Frames 7-11 mirror the first half onto the other side — weight on the character-left foot, character-right foot lifts and crosses behind, the character-left shoulder rides up and the head turns character-right to follow the step. The character-left forearm swings across in front of the torso and stays there through the hold. Frames 12-15 are the recovery: weight returns to centre then settles back over the character-right foot with both feet down, head squaring up again, so the last frame lands on the same planted, bounced-down pose frame 0 opens on. The seam is one natural bounce — do not add a settle.

For the artist agent

  • This is a motion source: it controls motion only. Character scale, proportions, identity, view and prop hand come from the manifest and approved authorities, never from this video.
  • Every side is named character-left / character-right (the performer's own sides). Screen sides are never used for anatomy. The mirror button flips the drawing only; the text does not change.
  • Playback is loop at 8 fps, 16 frames. Loop seam: clean (frame 16 cuts back to frame 0).
  • Depth is explicit: every landmark in pts is [x, y, z] with z negative toward the camera and the hips at zero, and each frame carries a depth block plus a cue sentence wherever the legs overlap. Sort bones by mean z and draw the far ones first; never guess which limb is in front.
  • Grounded frames keep the supporting heel on the canonical baseline; frames marked airborne leave it only through a coherent takeoff, arc and landing.
  • Draw keys first (pink), then pilots (blue), then in-betweens from locked neighbours. The frame notes below are the per-frame instruction set.
