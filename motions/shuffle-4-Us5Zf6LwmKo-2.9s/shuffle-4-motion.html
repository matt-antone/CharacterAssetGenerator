Performance arc

A two-beat cross-behind phrase danced almost entirely off the character-right foot. It opens on a held key: weight stacked on the character-right leg, character-left foot already off the floor, character-right forearm up near shoulder height as the anticipation. Through frames 1-5 the character-left knee folds tighter and tighter and the leg swings across and behind the standing leg while the character-right heel peels off the floor — the drive is the pelvis turning against the shoulders, not the foot.

Frames 6-13 are the recovery and the breath of the phrase: both feet come down onto their balls, heels lifted, legs long, arms hanging quiet. This is the stillest stretch in the loop and should read as a held settle, not as dead frames — the weight rocks between the balls of the feet while nothing else moves.

Frames 14-20 re-attack: the arms snap up and out, the character-left knee folds again and the cross repeats, deeper this time, with the character-right leg passing behind. Frames 21-23 unwind — legs straighten, arms drop long, and the weight begins transferring toward the character-left foot so the loop lands back on frame 0's stacked character-right stance with no visible cut.

For the artist agent

  • This is a motion source: it controls motion only. Character scale, proportions, identity, view and prop hand come from the manifest and approved authorities, never from this video.
  • Every side is named character-left / character-right (the performer's own sides). Screen sides are never used for anatomy. The mirror button flips the drawing only; the text does not change.
  • Playback is loop at 12 fps, 24 frames. Loop seam: clean (frame 24 cuts back to frame 0).
  • Depth is explicit: every landmark in pts is [x, y, z] with z negative toward the camera and the hips at zero, and each frame carries a depth block plus a cue sentence wherever the legs overlap. Sort bones by mean z and draw the far ones first; never guess which limb is in front.
  • Grounded frames keep the supporting heel on the canonical baseline; frames marked airborne leave it only through a coherent takeoff, arc and landing.
  • Draw keys first (pink), then pilots (blue), then in-betweens from locked neighbours. The frame notes below are the per-frame instruction set.
