Performance arc

A two-beat cutting-shapes phrase, one knee drive per beat, alternating sides, danced on the balls of both feet the whole way through. The engine is the knee, not the hips: each drive lifts the thigh fast to roughly waist height, folds the shin under, then the foot lowers and the weight rolls across to the other side. Nothing ever settles onto a flat heel, so every pose carries a light bounce.

Beat one runs 0-11. Frames 0-3 are the recovery of the previous character-left drive: the lifted knee unfolds and the foot searches for the floor while the torso stays upright. Frames 4-5 are the passing beat, weight centred over both balls, legs nearly straight, the only moment in the phrase with two feet down; play it light, it is a transit, not a stop. From 6 the character-right knee drives up and the shin swings behind the standing leg, while the character-right arm counters upward; frame 10 is the hit of the beat, the highest and most open pose in the set, and 11 holds it one frame before the release.

Beat two runs 12-23 and mirrors it. Frames 12-15 lower the character-right foot with the torso leaning character-right as follow-through, 16-17 are the second passing beat, and 18-22 drive the character-left knee up with the leg crossing behind the standing right. Frame 22 is the second hit, a touch quieter than 10, and 23 is already unfolding into frame 0, so the loop seam is mid-recovery with the character-right foot planted and the character-left knee still folded - no airborne frame at the cut.

For the artist agent

  • This is a motion source: it controls motion only. Character scale, proportions, identity, view and prop hand come from the manifest and approved authorities, never from this video.
  • Every side is named character-left / character-right (the performer's own sides). Screen sides are never used for anatomy. The mirror button flips the drawing only; the text does not change.
  • Playback is loop at 12 fps, 24 frames. Loop seam: clean (frame 24 cuts back to frame 0).
  • Depth is explicit: every landmark in pts is [x, y, z] with z negative toward the camera and the hips at zero, and each frame carries a depth block plus a cue sentence wherever the legs overlap. Sort bones by mean z and draw the far ones first; never guess which limb is in front.
  • Grounded frames keep the supporting heel on the canonical baseline; frames marked airborne leave it only through a coherent takeoff, arc and landing.
  • Draw keys first (pink), then pilots (blue), then in-betweens from locked neighbours. The frame notes below are the per-frame instruction set.
