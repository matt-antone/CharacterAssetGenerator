Pose grid

The pose grid the pose-grid command emits: traced frames as pose cards, four across and twelve per image, so a longer motion chunks across several. Photographs, not drawn figures — both rank the poses about equally, but drawn cards come back at 0.43–0.70 of the traced movement where photographs come back at 0.9–1.2. A consumer slices by the numbers declared in the manifest, never by measuring the picture. The character generator builds its own pose grid from the loose traced frames and does not read this one.

Performance arc

Hands frame the collarbones, elbows wide, weight on the character-right foot. From frame 2 they sweep down — past the ribs, across the waist at 3, low at the hips by 4 — while the weight rolls onto the character-left and the torso leans character-left to give the sweep somewhere to go.

Frames 5-7 recover, and the character-left shoulder lifting at 7 already anticipates the hands going back up. That anticipation is why this one closes on itself at 1.49x, the only clean seam in the set — it loops unattended with no blending.

For the artist agent

  • This is a motion source: it controls motion only. Character scale, proportions, identity, view and prop hand come from the manifest and approved authorities, never from this video.
  • Every side is named character-left / character-right (the performer's own sides). Screen sides are never used for anatomy. The mirror button flips the drawing only; the text does not change.
  • Playback is loop · out and back at 4 fps, 8 frames. Loop seam: clean (frame 7 cuts back to frame 0).
  • Depth is explicit: every landmark in pts is [x, y, z] with z negative toward the camera and the hips at zero, and each frame carries a depth block plus a cue sentence wherever the legs overlap. Sort bones by mean z and draw the far ones first; never guess which limb is in front.
  • Grounded frames keep the supporting heel on the canonical baseline; frames marked airborne leave it only through a coherent takeoff, arc and landing.
  • Draw keys first (pink), then pilots (blue), then in-betweens from locked neighbours. The frame notes below are the per-frame instruction set.
