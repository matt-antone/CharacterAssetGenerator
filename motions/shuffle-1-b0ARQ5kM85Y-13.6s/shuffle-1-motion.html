Performance arc

A two-second K-pop shuffle in four beats, danced square to a locked-off camera. The feet carry the rhythm and the arms answer it, and the engine underneath is the hips swinging against steady shoulders — she never travels and she never leaves the ground. Frame 0 is the home pose and the loop seam: weight centred, both heels lifted, forearms out to the side at chest height. One frame only; the wind-up for the leg lift is already in it.

Beat one (1–5): the weight drops onto the character-left ball and the character-right leg swings up and across, knee folding tight at frame 3 — the highest the foot travels all loop and the biggest shape in the set. The arms stay low and wide through it, counterweighting. Frames 4–5 are the fall, not a second hold. Beat two (6–9): the foot lands, weight settles over both feet, and the follow-through runs up into the arms — the character-left arm crosses in front of the torso while the character-right folds tight and rises to shoulder height. Feet stay planted and quiet here; the whole accent is upper body.

Beat three (10–15) is the weight change the rest hangs on: the torso leans character-right, the character-left hip rides up, and both arms drop low by the hips before starting to climb. Frame 12 is the deepest, most compressed point — the counterweight to the hit still coming. Beat four (16–23) unwinds it: the character-right leg passes behind the character-left, the hips turn character-right against the shoulders, and the arms sweep up and out to the hit at 20–21, both above the shoulders with elbows square and the body open and upright. Frames 22–23 are already the release — arms still high but falling, the character-left leg crossing behind, weight rolling onto the character-right foot. Cut 23→0 on the beat: arms drop, feet close, wind-up reloaded.

For the artist agent

  • This is a motion source: it controls motion only. Character scale, proportions, identity, view and prop hand come from the manifest and approved authorities, never from this video.
  • Every side is named character-left / character-right (the performer's own sides). Screen sides are never used for anatomy. The mirror button flips the drawing only; the text does not change.
  • Playback is loop at 12 fps, 24 frames. Loop seam: clean (frame 24 cuts back to frame 0).
  • Depth is explicit: every landmark in pts is [x, y, z] with z negative toward the camera and the hips at zero, and each frame carries a depth block plus a cue sentence wherever the legs overlap. Sort bones by mean z and draw the far ones first; never guess which limb is in front.
  • Grounded frames keep the supporting heel on the canonical baseline; frames marked airborne leave it only through a coherent takeoff, arc and landing.
  • Draw keys first (pink), then pilots (blue), then in-betweens from locked neighbours. The frame notes below are the per-frame instruction set.
