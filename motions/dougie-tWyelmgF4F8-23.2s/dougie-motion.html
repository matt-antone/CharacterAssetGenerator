Performance arc

A two-bar dougie groove, danced on the balls of both feet with the knees soft the whole way through. The engine is a side-to-side weight rock: the hips lead, the opposite shoulder drops toward the foot taking the weight, and the arms swing lazily across the body between hip height and chest height. Nothing locks; every extreme is a soft arrival that immediately gives back into the next rock.

Frames 0-3 open on the character-right foot with the character-left foot just off the floor and the character-right arm swinging up to chest height across the chest, the tail of the previous swipe. Frames 4-11 are the settle and the counter-swing: weight travels onto the character-left foot, the character-right arm drains down to waist height and the character-left arm rises across to chest, the character-right shoulder and hip lifting against it. Frame 10 is the high point of that swing.

Frames 12-17 are the hit of the phrase: the character-left knee picks up to about 90 degrees, the hips turn character-right against the shoulders, and frame 14 holds for a beat before the foot sets back down. Frames 18-23 recover into the next rock, arms back down past waist height, shoulders levelling, weight centring, and frame 23 hands straight back to frame 0 on the same bounce. Play the seam through: the cut is on a live step rather than a matched pose, so carry the downward momentum of 23 into the lift of 0 rather than easing to a stop.

For the artist agent

  • This is a motion source: it controls motion only. Character scale, proportions, identity, view and prop hand come from the manifest and approved authorities, never from this video.
  • Every side is named character-left / character-right (the performer's own sides). Screen sides are never used for anatomy. The mirror button flips the drawing only; the text does not change.
  • Playback is loop at 12 fps, 24 frames. Loop seam: needs blend (frame 24 cuts back to frame 0).
  • Depth is explicit: every landmark in pts is [x, y, z] with z negative toward the camera and the hips at zero, and each frame carries a depth block plus a cue sentence wherever the legs overlap. Sort bones by mean z and draw the far ones first; never guess which limb is in front.
  • Grounded frames keep the supporting heel on the canonical baseline; frames marked airborne leave it only through a coherent takeoff, arc and landing.
  • Draw keys first (pink), then pilots (blue), then in-betweens from locked neighbours. The frame notes below are the per-frame instruction set.
