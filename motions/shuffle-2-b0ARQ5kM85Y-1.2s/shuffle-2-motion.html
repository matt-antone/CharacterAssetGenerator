Performance arc

A two-second one-shot: a standing bounce that winds up into a turn and travels out of frame's centre. Read it as three beats, not a cycle — it starts square to camera and ends side-on, which is why it does not loop and is not cut as one.

Beat one (0–7) is the setup and the only still part: weight over both feet, heels lifted, both arms up and folded at about ninety degrees out to the sides, hands near shoulder height. The motion here is small and springy — a bounce in the knees and a little alternation in the hands, nothing travelling. Frames 1 and 3 are holds; play the whole run as one sustained pose with life in it rather than eight separate shapes.

Beat two (8–15) is the wind-up and the turn. The character-right arm reaches overhead at 9, the hands drop in toward the face through 10–11, and the body begins turning off-square. From 12 the character-left knee drives up high, folded tight, while the weight sits on the character-right ball — 12 to 14 is the biggest shape in the set and the moment the dance commits. Beat three (16–23) pays it off: fully side-on now, the arms extend forward and down in front of the chest, the legs alternate lifting behind, and the body carries round and back toward camera. Frame 23 is the terminal pose — hands up by the face, weight on the character-left foot. Hold it; nothing cuts back to frame 0.

For the artist agent

  • This is a motion source: it controls motion only. Character scale, proportions, identity, view and prop hand come from the manifest and approved authorities, never from this video.
  • Every side is named character-left / character-right (the performer's own sides). Screen sides are never used for anatomy. The mirror button flips the drawing only; the text does not change.
  • Playback is one-shot at 12 fps, 24 frames. Loop seam: n/a (frame 24 cuts back to frame 0).
  • Depth is explicit: every landmark in pts is [x, y, z] with z negative toward the camera and the hips at zero, and each frame carries a depth block plus a cue sentence wherever the legs overlap. Sort bones by mean z and draw the far ones first; never guess which limb is in front.
  • Grounded frames keep the supporting heel on the canonical baseline; frames marked airborne leave it only through a coherent takeoff, arc and landing.
  • Draw keys first (pink), then pilots (blue), then in-betweens from locked neighbours. The frame notes below are the per-frame instruction set.
